When a Korean ceremony begins, the first people to walk are neither the groom nor the bride but the parents. The minute or two in which both sets of parents walk the aisle sets the first impression of the day's music. Yet couples who spend weeks on the bride's entrance often leave this one to whatever the venue plays. Using the ceremony programme of ArtM Studio Sounds, which produces its own classical arrangements, here is how to choose music for the parents' entrance. (As of November 2026)

## What this piece does

It does three things: it draws the guests' attention to the aisle, it gives the parents a pulse to walk to without nerves, and it sets up a contrast with the entrances that follow. A good piece here **does not sound like the main event**. Warm and dignified, but one degree cooler than the bride's entrance.

![The first scene of the ceremony — the aisle of a 500-seat hall (a wedding venue on the Han River in Seoul)](/images/convention-wedding-bw.jpg)

## Four criteria

**1. A pulse that fits the walk.** Parents walk slowly. A piece at around 60–70 beats a minute is comfortable. A march hurries the step; something too slow makes it awkward.

**2. A length that fits the aisle.** The time depends on the aisle: 40 seconds in a small hall, more than a minute and a half on a long aisle. If the mothers' candle lighting follows, allow two to three minutes. Walk it and time it on your site visit.

**3. A piece that can fade anywhere.** The music has to come down when the entrance ends. A piece whose flow repeats, so that it can fade at any bar, is safer than one that builds to a late climax.

**4. No overlap with the other entrances.** Use Pachelbel's Canon for both the parents and the bride and the scenes blur. Write the three entrance pieces on one line and check that the mood rises step by step.

## Classical pieces that suit the parents' entrance

From the repertoire ArtM Studio Sounds covers in its ["21 pieces in ceremony order" video](https://youtu.be/ckgocda-R2Q) and its bridal entrance series, these carry over well to the parents' entrance by the criteria above.

| Piece | Mood | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bach, "Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring" | A gently flowing line, steady pulse | The safest choice. It fades naturally anywhere |
| Handel, "Largo" | Slow and stately | A high-ceilinged large hall, a formal ceremony |
| Elgar, "Salut d'Amour" | Warm and affectionate | A small wedding; both sets of parents entering together |
| Pachelbel, Canon in D | Calmly building harmony | A long entrance through to the candle lighting (if not used for the bride) |
| Bach, Prelude in C from The Well-Tempered Clavier | Clear and composed | Outdoor and garden weddings; spaces that suit a single piano |
| A short piece in the vein of Schumann's "Träumerei" | Quiet and lyrical | A ceremony without an officiant, a small hall |

In the same video ArtM Studio Sounds places the Triumphal March from Verdi's Aida at the groom's entrance, the Canon at the vows, Handel's Largo at the declaration, the Radetzky March at the re-entrance and The Blue Danube at the cake cutting. If you use the Largo or the Canon for the parents, change the other moment so nothing repeats.

## Choosing by situation

- **Only the mothers enter, to light candles**: allow two to three minutes. A piece with a repeating structure suits. Agree a signal with the sound operator to fade as the lighting ends.
- **All four parents enter together**: the step is a little quicker. Something bright like "Salut d'Amour" suits.
- **International couples**: the parents' entrance is unfamiliar to overseas family. A classical piece everyone knows says "someone important is entering" without explanation.
- **Live music**: piano or strings can match the length on the spot, which is safest. With a recording, prepare an edited file in advance.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| How long it takes to walk the aisle | It sets the length and the edit point |
| How audio is delivered (USB, file format) and the deadline | It prevents a file that will not play on the day |
| Who gives the cue to fade | The MC and sound operator make the scene together |
| Whether the piece can be played at the rehearsal | The same piece sounds different in each hall |
| Whether live music is possible and an instrument is available | The length can be matched on the spot |

For example, at Sevit Island on the Han River in Seoul, ArtM Studio Sounds, part of the same group, produces music for each part of the ceremony, and entrance routes and music cues are matched together at the rehearsal on the day. At any venue, ask whether you can have the list of pieces from the parents' entrance to the recessional on one page.

## Summary

1. A piece that does not sound like the main event, one degree cooler than the bride's entrance
2. A pulse for a slow walk, a length for the aisle, a structure that fades anywhere
3. Three entrance pieces that do not overlap and rise step by step
4. Check the rights in the arrangement and recording, and play it at the rehearsal
