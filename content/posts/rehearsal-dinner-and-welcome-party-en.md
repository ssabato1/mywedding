A Korean wedding is over in a day, in two or three hours. A wedding abroad often begins the evening before: the rehearsal dinner that follows the ceremony rehearsal, and the welcome party that greets guests who have travelled. When guests are flying in, the evening before is not an extra but part of the welcome. Here is what the two events are, how they differ, and how to include them in a wedding in Korea. The full preparation for overseas guests is covered first in [Hosting 50 Overseas Guests](/blog/hosting-50-overseas-guests-en).

## What the rehearsal dinner is

The rehearsal dinner is a meal held after the ceremony rehearsal. The American venue guide Here Comes The Guide, in its [rehearsal dinner etiquette](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-party-ideas/detail/rehearsal-dinner-etiquette), calls it "the party before the party".

In the English-speaking world the wedding party, the children in the procession and the officiant rehearse the entrance the day before. Those gathered for the rehearsal then dine together. On the wedding day the couple has no time for a long conversation with anyone, so this is the one occasion to sit quietly with those closest to them.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| When | The evening before the wedding, straight after the rehearsal |
| Host | Traditionally the groom's parents. Today the couple often pay themselves, or the families share |
| Guests | The wedding party, flower girls and ring bearers with their guardians, the officiant, close family and friends |
| Invitations | Separate from the wedding invitation, about four weeks before. An online invitation is acceptable |
| Place | A restaurant or private room near the rehearsal site |

Here Comes The Guide adds that whoever pays has the last word on the details, and advises booking the place three to six months ahead.

## What the welcome party is

The welcome party is a relaxed gathering to greet the wedding guests. The American wedding publication The Wed, in its [welcome party etiquette](https://thewed.com/magazine/wedding-welcome-party-etiquette), describes it as the guests' first taste of the wedding and a chance to get to know one another. It is less formal and more laid-back than the other events.

The difference from the rehearsal dinner is who is invited.

| | Rehearsal dinner | Welcome party |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guests | Closest family and friends, and those with a role in the ceremony | Everyone invited to the wedding |
| Mood | A seated meal with toasts | A standing, mingling gathering |
| Purpose | A meal for those who have just rehearsed | Welcoming those who travelled and introducing them to each other |
| Dress | Smart | Casual is a safe choice |

The Wed notes three possibilities: the welcome party can replace the rehearsal dinner, follow straight after it, or be held on a separate day.

## When guests come from abroad

At a destination wedding the rule changes. Here Comes The Guide notes that destination weddings usually invite everyone attending to a pre-wedding event. Guests who have flown in are not left alone in their hotel the evening before.

There are two ways to do it.

- **One event.** Skip the rehearsal dinner and combine everything into one welcome party for overseas guests and close family.
- **Two stages.** Family and the wedding party dine first, and the other guests join towards the end at the bar or lounge of the same place. This is what Here Comes The Guide suggests.

The Wed advises weighing three things when setting the time: whether there is a separate rehearsal dinner, when overseas guests' flights arrive, and whether guests have time to rest. A late night is hard on someone who has just come off a long flight.

## Bringing it to a wedding in Korea

A Korean wedding has no rehearsal the day before. So rather than a "rehearsal dinner", it is more natural to think of **a dinner on the eve that doubles as the meeting of the two families**, or **a welcome dinner for overseas guests**.

- **Make it the first long meeting of the two families.** At an international wedding the two sets of parents sometimes meet for the first time on the wedding day. A meal the evening before makes the day itself much easier.
- **Decide who will interpret.** Seat a bilingual relative or friend close to both sets of parents.
- **Make the meal an introduction to Korea.** For overseas guests, Korean food becomes a memory of the trip. Ask in advance about foods to avoid and dietary needs.
- **Explain the next day.** A Korean wedding is unfamiliar to overseas guests. Explaining the gift of money, the pyebaek and how the meal works spares them confusion on the day.
- **Finish early.** The couple and both families start preparing at dawn. Aim to end before nine in the evening.

## Toasts and the order of the evening

The evening before is freer than the wedding day. A common order:

1. The host welcomes everyone and proposes a toast.
2. The meal.
3. Family and friends speak. Give the chance to those who will not speak at the wedding.
4. The couple give their thanks and go through the next day's schedule.

Two to three minutes each, from three or four people, is about right. Where two languages are mixed, keep the toasts short and interpret paragraph by paragraph, or place a printed translation at each seat.

Here Comes The Guide advises reserving a private room if you plan games or a slideshow. A film of childhood photographs of the two of you helps the families get to know each other.

## The timeline

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 3 to 6 months before | Book the place, close to where overseas guests are staying |
| With the invitations | Announce on the website or a details card that there is an event the evening before |
| 4 weeks before | Send the separate invitation and collect replies |
| 2 weeks before | Confirm numbers and dietary needs with the restaurant |
| 1 week before | Settle who speaks and in what order, the interpreting and the seating |

If only some guests are invited, do not enclose the invitation with the wedding invitation. Here Comes The Guide is clear that the rehearsal dinner invitation should never go in the same envelope, because uninvited guests would learn of it. ## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Place | A small banquet room or a private room at a restaurant suits it better than the wedding hall. Check the travel time from the guests' hotel |
| Flights and jet lag | Collect arrival times and check whether an event on the evening of arrival is too much for anyone |
| Food | Ask about foods to avoid and halal or vegetarian needs, and tell the restaurant |
| Sharing the cost | Have the two families agree in advance who hosts and pays. Customs differ by country |
| Getting back | Explain transport to the hotel after dinner, including late-night public transport and taxis |
| The next day | Repeat the ceremony time, meeting point and shuttle times in print or by message |

The evening before does not make the wedding a day longer. It is the time when those who came from far away learn each other's faces, and the two families first sit at one table. With that evening behind them, the guests at the wedding are no longer strangers but people who have already said hello.
