A bouquet consultation usually opens with "which flowers do you like?" British florists' guides reverse the order. First the dress decides the **shape and size**, the season decides the **flowers**, the backdrop of the venue decides the **colour**, and only then do you add one flower you love. Here it is in that order.

## 1. The dress decides shape and size

London's [Blooming Haus guide](https://bloominghaus.com/wedding-flowers/best-bouquet-shape-for-wedding-dress/) pairs bouquets with silhouettes like this.

| Dress | Bouquet | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-line | Medium hand-tied; a V shape with a V-neck | Medium is the safest proportion. A small bouquet lets the dress lead |
| Sheath, column | A round bouquet or long-stemmed, one flower in one colour | Contrasts with the vertical line, or lengthens it |
| Ball gown | Large and small flowers mixed, finished with a wide ribbon | A small bouquet is lost in the skirt; large flowers alone look heavy |
| Mermaid, trumpet | Small with an embellished dress; cascade with a plain one | Draws the eye down the skirt |
| Tea length, short | A small posy, or a single stem with long ribbon | A large bouquet breaks the proportion of a short skirt |

THE WED's [bouquet guide](https://thewed.com/magazine/ultimate-guide-to-choosing-a-bridal-bouquet) adds one principle: **a showy bouquet with a simple dress, a simple bouquet with a showy dress.** Carry a large bouquet with heavy beading or lace and neither is seen.

![A medium hand-tied bouquet — the safest proportion for A-line and soft A-line. Peonies, garden roses and clematis with fern](/images/bouquet-01.jpg)

## 2. The season decides the flowers

Flowers in season cost less, are in better condition and have not travelled far. Overseas guides agree on choosing what grows that season. For Korea:

- **Spring (March–May)**: tulips, ranunculus, peonies (May), sweet peas, anemones. They suit light, round shapes.

![A spring bouquet — tulips, sweet peas and clematis loosely hand-tied](/images/bouquet-06.jpg)

- **Summer (June–August)**: hydrangea, lisianthus, roses, delphinium. Choose flowers that stand the heat; peonies and tulips do not last an hour outdoors at midday.
- **Autumn (September–November)**: dahlias, disbud chrysanthemums, roses, pampas and grasses. Colours deepen, so terracotta and burgundy work well.

![An autumn bouquet — nerines and tulips gathered in a single red. Most vivid against a white dress and a dark hall](/images/bouquet-05.jpg)

- **Winter (December–February)**: amaryllis, hellebores, roses, eucalyptus and conifer greens. White against dark green photographs sharply.

For an outdoor wedding, ask first about **how long the flowers last**. If you will hold the bouquet for three hours or more through ceremony, photographs and party, roses and lisianthus are safer than ranunculus.

## 3. The backdrop decides the colour

Match the bouquet's colour not to the dress but to the **photographic backdrop**. The dress is white in any case; what sits behind the bouquet in photographs is the venue's wall, glass and sky.

- **Glass wall or river (blue and grey)**: warm colours come alive. Peach, apricot, cream, pale terracotta.

![Peach tones for a river or glass-wall backdrop — garden roses, hellebores and sweet peas](/images/bouquet-02.jpg)

- **Lawn or garden (green)**: colours that do not merge with green. White, pale pink, lavender, or the opposite — vivid orange and coral.

![Lavender tones for a garden backdrop — lavender, roses and eucalyptus, clear against a green lawn](/images/bouquet-03.jpg)

- **Night view and lighting (dark)**: white and bright pastels read most clearly. Deep burgundy photographs black at night.

![Bright pastels for night and lighting — a small round bouquet of pale apricot garden roses with white blossom](/images/bouquet-04.jpg)

- **Large LED and indoor lighting**: the screen's colour reflects onto the bouquet. Mostly white with one accent colour is safe.

According to Belmond's 2026 trend roundup, searches for "colourful bridal bouquet" on Hitched in the UK rose 267%. The white bouquet as default is giving way to colour chosen for the backdrop.

## 4. Last, one flower you love

This is the finish the overseas guides share. Once the three criteria have set the frame, add one or two flowers with meaning — from your mother's wedding bouquet, the flowers at the proposal, the national flower of your partner's country — and the bouquet becomes yours. An international couple might add one flower from each country.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Colour of the hall's walls, glass and floor | Colour follows the backdrop. Photograph it on the site visit and send it to the florist |
| How long you will hold it, from ceremony to party | Three hours or more calls for flowers that resist heat and wilting |
| Whether it is outdoors and windy | A long cascade swings in the wind; a round hand-tied bouquet is safer outdoors |
| Whether the venue has its own florist | Venue flowers and bouquet can be matched in colour |
| Whether there will be a bouquet toss | A separate small toss bouquet lets you keep your own |

![A small bouquet for each bridesmaid — sharing them out instead of a toss is growing too (the terrace of a wedding venue on the Han River in Seoul)](/images/bridesmaids-bouquets-01.jpg)

For example, at venues where river and sky are the backdrop, such as the Sevit Island rooftop and the Marina Park garden on the Han River in Seoul, peach and cream bouquets photograph best, and Anniversary Flower, part of the same group, matches the venue flowers and the bouquet together. At any venue, ask first whether the colours match if décor and bouquet come from the same florist.

## Summary

1. Dress silhouette → bouquet shape and size (showy with a simple dress, simple with a showy one)
2. Season → flowers (in season; flowers that last if outdoors)
3. Backdrop → colour (matched to wall, glass and sky, not the dress)
4. One flower you love
