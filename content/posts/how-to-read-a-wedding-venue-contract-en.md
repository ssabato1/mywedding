Once a venue tour ends and you have found the place you like, the contract is usually put in front of you the same day. In the excitement it is easy to check only the price and the date and sign, but the problems that appear later almost always come from the small print on the back. Drawing on contract guides from The Knot and Here Comes The Guide, here is what to read in a Korean wedding venue contract. This article is general guidance, not legal advice.

## First: does it say what you are getting?

In The Knot's [guide to wedding venue contracts](https://www.theknot.com/content/ceremony-contract-points), events lawyer Leah Weinberg says that when you are spending this much, the most important thing is to know exactly what you are getting. The basics the guide lists are:

- **Date and hours**: start and end times, and the time from which you may enter.
- **Spaces**: which areas are yours, such as the hall, bridal room, lobby and terrace.
- **What is included**: an itemised list of what the venue provides, from staging and sound to flowers and staff.
- **Capacity**: that your guest count is permitted in that space.
- **House rules**: whether petals, candles or sparklers are allowed.

A promise made in conversation is only a promise once it is in the contract. If you were told that something would be included as a courtesy, ask for it to be written in the remarks.

## The guaranteed number: you pay on this figure, not on attendance

The centre of a Korean venue contract is the **guaranteed number**. Meals are charged on the figure in the contract, not on how many guests arrive. Check four things.

1. The **minimum guarantee**.
2. **When the final number is fixed.** Usually around ten days before the wedding, after which it often cannot be reduced.
3. If **fewer** guests come: you are charged for the guarantee.
4. If **more** guests come: up to what margin food is prepared, and how the extra is charged.

Here Comes The Guide's [guide to venue contracts](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-ideas/wedding-venue-contracts) also advises checking for minimum guest counts or food and beverage minimums, and for the date by which the final headcount is due.

## Deposit, interim payment and balance: when, how much, how

The Knot's guide recommends listing the deposit and each instalment with its amount, due date, payee and accepted payment methods. Here Comes The Guide adds that you should check for card surcharges and for what happens if a payment is late.

- The amount of the deposit, and whether paying it confirms the date.
- **How many days before the wedding** the interim payment and balance are due. This varies by venue.
- When items settled on actual quantities on the day, such as food and drink, are paid.
- Whether the quoted figures **include VAT**.
- Receipts, tax invoices and payment methods.

## Cancellation and changes: the clause to read most carefully

This is the part both guides stress most. Check whether the deposit is refundable, what the cancellation charges are, and whether money already paid moves to a new date if you reschedule.

- **Cancelling soon after signing**: whether the deposit is refunded within a set period, and how many days that is.
- **Cancelling later**: charges usually rise in steps as the date approaches. Copy the bands and percentages into a table of your own.
- **Changing the date**: how many times, and until how many months before. Whether benefits given at the original contract are kept.
- **Refund timing**: when a refund is actually paid.

Korea's Fair Trade Commission publishes consumer dispute resolution standards that include the wedding industry. If a cancellation clause seems heavy, look up the current standard and compare before you sign.

## Extra charges and what you may bring in

The Knot recommends getting the full list of additional fees in writing. Overtime when music runs past the agreed time is the usual example.

- **Overtime**: what it costs if the wedding runs long, and in what increments.
- **Outside vendors**: whether you may bring your own photographer, videographer, florist or make-up artist, or only listed vendors, and whether there is a fee.
- **Food and alcohol**: whether bringing them in is prohibited or allowed on conditions.
- **Delivery times**: when a photo table or favours may be brought in and removed.
- **Damage**: how damage caused by guests is handled.

## Weather and force majeure

- **Force majeure**: what happens when the wedding cannot take place for a reason that is nobody's fault, such as a natural disaster. See whether the clause only releases both sides, or also allows postponement or a refund.
- **Rain plan**: for an outdoor wedding, whether the indoor alternative, the decision time and any extra cost are in the contract. The Knot's list has no separate item for rain, so for an outdoor wedding you need to ask for this to be written in.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Whether the time slot is generous (three hours or more), and the overtime charge | So the running order is not cut or rushed |
| The minimum guarantee and the date the final number is fixed | The basis of the meal charge |
| The margin and charge for extra guests | Covers guests who arrive unannounced |
| Due dates and payment methods for deposit, interim payment and balance | Put the payment dates in your calendar |
| The refund period and the cancellation bands | The clause with the most money at stake |
| Conditions for changing the date, and whether benefits are kept | Terms can change after a reschedule |
| Rules on outside vendors and items brought in | Your photography and flower plans depend on them |
| Rain plan and force majeure | The safeguard for an outdoor wedding |
| Verbal promises written into the contract | It is a promise once it is written |

Some venues now email the full contract conditions together with the quote. Asking for time to read them at home before you sign is not impolite.

## Summary

- Look first not at the price but at what you receive, when and where.
- Meals are charged on the guaranteed number. Check the cut-off date and how over and under are settled.
- Copy the cancellation and change terms into a table of dates and percentages.
- Ask for anything promised in conversation to be written into the contract.
