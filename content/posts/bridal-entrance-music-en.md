When the doors open and the bride takes her first step, what the guests hear is music. The entrance piece is the one that most people listen to most quietly, and the sound that stays in the film for life. Based on the ten bridal entrance pieces of ArtM Studio Sounds, which produces its own classical arrangements, here is the character of each piece and how to choose. (As of November 2026)

## How not to go wrong

**1. Match the size of the hall.** In a hall with a high ceiling and a long aisle, a big-sounding piece such as the Trumpet Voluntary or the Bridal Chorus fills the space. In a small hall or garden the same piece is too much; something a single piano can carry, like "Salut d'Amour" or the Nocturne, fits.

**2. Time the walk.** The bride's entrance usually takes a minute to a minute and a half. The best part of the piece has to fall within it. "Liebestraum" peaks late, so using only the opening can feel flat — decide the edit point in advance.

**3. Match the dress and the step.** A long train and full skirt slow the walk, and a slow piece suits. For a couple entering together in lighter dress, something brisk like "The Arrival of the Queen of Sheba" works.

**4. Decide the moment the doors open.** Whether the doors open on the first note or after an introduction changes the scene. The Canon gains from doors opening after the harmony has built; the Bridal Chorus wants the doors open on the first note.

**5. Let the entrances rise in steps.** A gentle piece for the parents, a strong one for the groom, the most beautiful of the day for the bride. Play the three in sequence and check nothing repeats.

## Widening to the whole ceremony

The bride's entrance is one piece of the ceremony's music. ArtM Studio Sounds' ["21 pieces in ceremony order"](https://youtu.be/ckgocda-R2Q) runs from the guests' arrival to the cake: quiet pieces by Satie, Bach, Debussy and Schumann before the ceremony; the Triumphal March from Verdi's Aida for the groom; Pachelbel's Canon at the vows; Handel's Largo at the declaration; the Bach–Gounod "Ave Maria" and Liszt's "Liebestraum" where a song would go; Mendelssohn's Wedding March for the recessional; the Radetzky March for the re-entrance; The Blue Danube for the cake; and Boccherini's Minuet and Mozart's Eine kleine Nachtmusik during the meal.

If you choose the Canon for the bride's entrance, change the piece at the vows, so each piece belongs to one scene only.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Who handles the doors and the music cue | First note and first step have to meet |
| How long it takes to walk the aisle | It sets the edit point |
| The hall's acoustics (reverberation, speaker positions) | The same piece sounds different in each hall |
| Whether the piece can be played at the rehearsal | So the day is not the first time you hear it |
| Whether live music is possible | The length can follow the step |
| Rights in the arrangement and recording | The work may be public domain; the recording has rights |

For example, at Sevit Island on the Han River in Seoul, ArtM Studio Sounds, part of the same group, produces the ceremony music, and entrance routes, microphones and music cues are matched together at the rehearsal on the day. Before choosing, listen to the previews on the [ArtM Studio Sounds website](https://artmstudiosounds.com) and [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/artmstudiosounds/).

## Summary

1. Traditional: the Bridal Chorus and the Canon; brilliant: the two trumpet pieces; affectionate: "Salut d'Amour" and the Nocturne
2. Match the size of the hall, the length of the walk and the weight of the dress
3. Match the moment the doors open to the first note
4. Three entrance pieces that do not overlap, played at the rehearsal
