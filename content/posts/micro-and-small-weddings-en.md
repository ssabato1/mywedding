"We want it small" is no longer only about budget. US data show weddings shrinking steadily for twenty years; half the reason is money and the other half is time. Using overseas statistics and reporting, here is why small weddings are growing and what needs closer attention the smaller you go. (As of November 2026)

## The change in numbers

- The average US guest count fell from 184 in 2006 to **131** in 2024. Weddings of 50 or fewer rose from 10% in 2013 to **18%** in 2024. ([Axios, Why weddings are shrinking](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/29/small-wedding-price-benefits), citing The Wedding Report)
- The Knot's [2026 Real Weddings Study](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study) puts the average for couples married in 2025 at **117 guests**, with spending per guest at $292, up on the year before. Fewer people, more spent on each.
- The wedding platform Joy writes in its [2026 trend roundup](https://withjoy.com/blog/2026-wedding-trends-what-couples-are-actually-doing-this-year/) that many couples are cutting from 200 to 80–100.

Overseas, a "micro wedding" usually means 50 or fewer and a "small wedding" 50–100. In Korea a wedding of 100 is often called small, so it is clearer to talk to venues in numbers.

## Four reasons weddings are shrinking

**1. Cost.** McMurray of The Wedding Report, quoted by Axios, says the main reason couples go small is to spend less. Food, venue and invitations are all priced per head, so cutting guests is the surest way to cut cost. The industry reckons a micro wedding can cost less than half a traditional one.

**2. Stress.** A small wedding has less to prepare, and the budget saved can go on what the couple actually wants — photography, food, travel.

**3. Time.** Fewer guests means more time with each. The trade magazine [Hotels Magazine](https://hotelsmag.com/news/why-small-personalized-weddings-are-driving-demand-in-2026/) writes that "small and personal" weddings are driving demand in 2026, with couples moving budget from logistics to experiences that tell their own story.

**4. Taste.** City hall and courthouse weddings have become a style, and Gen Z searches for "city hall elopement" on Pinterest have surged. Going small is a choice, not a compromise.

## What a small wedding asks more of

Paradoxically, the smaller the wedding, the more particular the venue requirements. At a wedding for 200 the running order and food make the mood; at a wedding for 40, **space and time** do.

- **The space has to fit.** Seat 40 in a hall for 300 and it looks empty however it is dressed. Ask first for the minimum guarantee and for photographs of the room set small.
- **It needs time.** The strength of a small wedding is conversation, and conversation takes time. Overseas, micro weddings usually run four to five hours from ceremony to dinner.
- **The running order has to be free.** With 40 guests you can replace the officiant's address with a word from each guest. A small wedding shows its best where the order can be adjusted.
- **You should be able to raise the spend per head.** As the overseas data show, the point of a small wedding is not spending less but spending more on each person. A venue with options — a course upgrade, live music, welcome drinks — suits it.

![A small wedding with every guest at one long table (a wedding venue on the Han River in Seoul)](/images/rive-gauche-small-wedding-01.jpg)

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum guarantee | Whether you can contract for 40–60. Many halls take 100 or more only |
| A space scaled to the guest count | A small dedicated hall or garden that does not look empty |
| A slot of three hours or more | Conversation is the main event of a small wedding |
| Whether the running order can be changed | Your own order in place of an address or song |
| Per-head options | Whether course, drinks and music can be upgraded |
| A link to an after-party | Whether the evening carries on in the same place |

For example, the Marina Park garden on the Han River in Seoul can be booked from a minimum guarantee of 50, and the ceremony leads into dinner and an after-party in the same space. When choosing a venue, ask "can we use it unhurried for three hours or more with 50 guests?" and you will know at once whether it suits a small wedding.
