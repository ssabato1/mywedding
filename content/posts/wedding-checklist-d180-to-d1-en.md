Wedding planning is hard not because there is so much to do but because nobody tells you what to do first. Below is one page covering the schedule most Korean venues share, from six months out to the day before. Deadlines differ a little from venue to venue, so start by copying the dates in your contract into this table.

## D-180 — Set the frame

- **Type of wedding**: indoor convention hall, outdoor garden or small wedding. Deciding what kind of day you want before the guest count makes choosing a venue faster.
- **Date and venue contract**: popular spring and autumn weekends have few free dates even six months out. Check the length of the slot, whether another wedding follows, the wet-weather alternative, the minimum guarantee, the deposit and balance schedule, and the cancellation terms.
- **Budget split**: start with the big items — venue, food, dress, photography, flowers, music. In overseas surveys more than half of couples said their first budget was lower than the real cost. Keep 10–15% in reserve.
- **First guest list**: it need not be exact. Just check it fits between the venue's minimum and maximum.

## D-150 — Choose the people

- **Start dress and tuxedo consultations**: four to five months ahead for made-to-measure, three for rental. Telling the shop the type of venue (outdoor, glass-walled, large LED) changes the silhouette they recommend.
- **Photography and video**: photographers in the style you want (documentary, film look, classic) book out early.
- **MC and officiant, or a secular celebrant**: an international couple should confirm now whether English–Korean bilingual MCing is available.
- **Finalise invitation wording and design**: printing at D-90 and posting at D-60 is the norm.

## D-90 — First meeting with the venue

- **Meeting with the venue coordinator**: most venues hold the first detailed meeting now. Settle the draft running order, table layout, sound and video use, rules on outside flowers and access times for outside vendors.
- **Menu tasting**: after the tasting, confirm the course and the alternatives (halal, vegetarian, allergy). If you have overseas guests, collect dietary requests now.
- **Direction for flowers and décor**: where the venue already has a strong backdrop (outdoors, a glass wall), use fewer flowers and concentrate them in one spot.
- **Send information to overseas guests**: recommended accommodation, how to get there from the airport or express bus terminal, whether there is a shuttle, the dress code. The English guide has to go out now to match flight bookings.

## D-60 — Invitations out, running order fixed

- **Send invitations**: paper and mobile together. Set the reply deadline at D-21.
- **Fix the running order**: entrances, vows, song, toast, cake cutting, greeting the parents, recessional. Be bold about cutting.
- **Music list**: entrance, processional, recessional and reception background. For live music, hand the list to the musicians.
- **First dress fitting**, tuxedo fitting.

![D-60, deciding the bouquet and flower colours](/images/bouquet-02.jpg)

## D-30 — Get the numbers right

- **Tally the replies**: attending, not attending, plus-ones.
- **Draft the seating plan**: group both families, overseas guests and friends by table. Seating one English-speaking relative or friend at each overseas table changes the mood.
- **Send the vendor list**: tell the venue the access times and vehicle numbers of the photographer, videographer, florist and make-up artist.
- **Order favours, the photo table and the welcome board.**

## D-10 — Confirm the guaranteed number

- **Final guaranteed number**: most venues fix the guarantee now. Meals are prepared and the balance calculated from this figure, so telephone anyone who has not replied.
- **Print the programme and seating chart**, share the MC's final script.
- **Final dress fitting**, make-up rehearsal.

## D-2 — Balance and final checks

- **Pay the balance**: recheck the deadline in the contract. It usually falls two or three days after the guarantee is fixed.
- **Final check with the venue**: arrival time, bridal room access, sound and video files (on USB and in the cloud), rehearsal time for singers and musicians.
- **Wet-weather check**: for an outdoor wedding, look at the forecast the day before and agree with the venue the criteria and timing for moving indoors.

![D-10: once the guarantee is in, only the weather is left (a wedding venue on the Han River in Seoul)](/images/marinapark-ceremony-05.jpg)

## D-1 — A day of rest

- Put everything in one bag: rings, vows, marriage registration papers (if filing on the day), cash and envelopes, basic medicine, spare tights and insoles, chargers.
- If overseas family has arrived, share a short dinner. Avoid a long evening and simply repeat tomorrow's arrival time.
- The couple usually arrives 90 minutes before the ceremony. It varies by venue, so keep to the time given at the D-2 check.

## One-page summary

| When | Must be finished |
| --- | --- |
| D-180 | Type of wedding, date and venue contract, budget, first guest list |
| D-150 | Dress and tuxedo, photography and video, MC, invitation design |
| D-90 | Venue meeting, tasting and menu, floral direction, overseas guest information |
| D-60 | Invitations sent, running order and music fixed, first fitting |
| D-30 | Replies tallied, seating plan, vendor list, favours ordered |
| D-10 | Guaranteed number, programme and seating chart, final fitting |
| D-2 | Balance, final venue check, wet-weather decision |
| D-1 | One bag, a short family dinner, rest |

The D-90 meeting, D-10 guarantee and D-2 balance in this table are the schedule one wedding venue in Seoul actually uses, but most venues move at similar intervals. When you receive your contract, write the dates into this table and put it on the fridge.
