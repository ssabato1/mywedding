A guest list feels like a question of feelings, but in practice it is a question of numbers. Its length decides the size of the hall, the number of meals and half of the budget. Drawing on guides from Zola and WedSites, here is the order in which to build, trim and manage it.

## Step 1: decide who decides

Zola's [guide to making a guest list](https://zola.com/expert-advice/how-to-make-a-wedding-guest-list) advises settling who has a say before any names are written. Usually that is the couple and any parents who are contributing to the cost. Then:

1. Collect the names everyone involved would like to invite.
2. Merge them into one list.
3. Trim it to fit the budget and the capacity of the hall.
4. Record addresses and replies in a spreadsheet or a guest list tool.

In Korea the first question is usually how to divide the list between the two families. There is no fixed answer, but counting the groom's side, the bride's side and the couple's own friends and colleagues separately, and then balancing them within the hall's capacity, causes the fewest arguments.

## Step 2: one rule for trimming, applied to everyone

Zola's principle is that once you set a rule, you apply it without exceptions.

- **Children**: if the wedding is adults-only, it is adults-only for every family. One exception will upset the others.
- **Plus-ones**: limit them to spouses and long-term partners, and apply that evenly too.
- **Colleagues**: decide whether to invite the whole team or only a few close ones, and hold the line.

## Step 3: the A list and the B list

This is the method for when there are not enough seats. According to WedSites' [guide to A and B lists](https://blog.wedsites.com/how-to-use-a-and-b-lists-for-your-wedding-invitations/), invitations go to the A list about 12 weeks before the wedding, and as declines free up places, to the B list by about 8 weeks before. Any later and the invitation can feel like a late addition.

There is etiquette to observe. Do not split a close group of friends between A and B, because they will quickly find out who was invited first.

## Step 4: collect replies, and phone those who do not answer

Korean invitations often do not ask for a reply, so attendance is estimated by feel. Simply using the RSVP function of a mobile invitation and stating a deadline reduces the error considerably.

- Set the reply deadline one to two weeks before the day you must give the venue your guaranteed number.
- After the deadline, contact everyone who has not replied. For guests on the parents' side, ask the parents to confirm.
- Keep attendance, declines, companions and dietary needs (vegetarian, halal, allergies) in one table.

## Step 5: no-shows and the guaranteed number

Some people who said yes will not come, and some will arrive unannounced. So keep two numbers separately.

- **Guaranteed number**: the minimum number of meals in your contract. You are charged for it even if fewer people attend.
- **Expected number**: your real estimate from the replies.

It is safest to set the guarantee close to the confirmed replies, slightly on the conservative side, and to ask the venue in advance how many extra meals can be served on the day. Many venues prepare a margin above the guarantee.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| The hall's minimum guarantee and maximum capacity | They set the floor and ceiling of the list |
| When the guaranteed number is fixed | Count back from this date to set the reply deadline |
| How many extra meals are possible on the day | Covers guests who arrive unannounced |
| How a shortfall is charged | No-shows are still charged up to the guarantee |
| The deadline for dietary alternatives | Vegetarian, halal and allergy meals need numbers in advance |
| Whether assigned seating is possible | Overseas guests and older relatives can be seated together |

## Summary

- Decide who decides before writing any names.
- Use one rule for trimming and apply it to everyone.
- A list at 12 weeks, B list by 8 weeks. Do not split close groups.
- Close RSVPs one to two weeks before the guarantee is due, and contact those who have not replied.
