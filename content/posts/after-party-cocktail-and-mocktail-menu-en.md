When dinner ends and the music changes, guests gather at the bar. Two things make an after-party: the music and the bar. But the longer the menu, the longer the queue and the more bottles left over. Drawing on guides from THE WED and Wezoree, here is how to keep the menu short and clear. Prices are not covered.

## How much: one drink per guest per hour

THE WED's [guide to how much alcohol a wedding needs](https://thewed.com/magazine/how-much-alcohol-you-actually-need-for-your-wedding) gives a simple basis.

- About one drink per person per hour.
- Assume about 75% of guests will drink. More for an adults-only evening party, fewer for a daytime event or a mixed-age crowd.
- Add 10–15% to the figure you calculate.

Wezoree's [wedding bar menu guide](https://wezoree.com/inspiration/nailing-your-wedding-bar-menu-start-finish-guide) counts about five glasses to a bottle of wine. For 100 guests and a two-hour party the basis is about 200 drinks, with the share of those who do not drink moved to mocktails and soft drinks.

## The line-up: wine, beer and two signature drinks

Both guides recommend a **limited bar**. Not every spirit, but wine, beer and one or two signature cocktails the couple has chosen.

- **Two or three base spirits.** Wezoree suggests keeping to familiar ones such as vodka, rum and tequila. More types mean more unused bottles.
- **Give them names.** A name taken from the two of you or the place you met turns the menu into a story.
- **Put up a menu board.** Listing each cocktail and its ingredients at the bar speeds up ordering.
- **Leave out very sweet drinks.** Pre-made fruit punch is not needed unless guests ask for it.

## Mocktails: with the same care

More guests than you expect will not drink: drivers, expectant mothers, those who abstain for religious or health reasons, older relatives and children. Wezoree advises making mocktails with the same attention as the signature cocktails.

- Offer an alcohol-free version of each signature cocktail. In the same glass with the same garnish, it looks no different in the toast photographs.
- Prepare sparkling water infused with fruit and herbs, and sodas flavoured with syrups.
- Mixers such as grapefruit soda, ginger ale, lemonade and cranberry juice serve both cocktails and mocktails.
- Give the alcohol-free station its own menu board.

## Matching the season

Wezoree's suggestions by season:

| Season | Wine and beer | Cocktails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | Crisp white wine, sangria, wheat beer | Sparkling cocktails, berries, citrus, cucumber, elderflower |
| Cool | Full-bodied red wine, stout | Whiskey cocktails, mulled wine, spiced apple cider, Irish coffee |

For an outdoor evening party in spring or autumn, you can divide the night: something cold and sparkling before sunset, and one warm drink added after dark.

## Food: bite-sized

A bar with only drinks does not last. Bite-sized canapés that can be eaten standing, placed beside the bar, keep guests from leaving.

## What to check when applying this in Korea

| What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Bar hours and closing time | This sets the length of the party |
| Whether there is a bartender | A signature cocktail needs someone to make it |
| Whether you may bring your own alcohol, and on what terms | Rules differ by venue |
| Whether alcohol-free options are standard | They should be ready for the toast as well |
| Whether the couple can set the signature menu | Agree names and ingredients in advance |
| Whether the slot is three hours or more | There has to be time for a party after dinner |
| How late music and sound may run | The bar and the music end together |

At one wedding venue on the Han River in Seoul, for example, the after-party can continue at the bar and terrace in the same building. At any venue, ask how late the bar stays open before you plan the menu.

## Summary

- Quantity: one drink per person per hour, plus a 10–15% margin.
- Menu: wine, beer and one or two signature cocktails.
- Mocktails get the same glass and the same care as cocktails.
- One seasonal drink and bite-sized food keep the party going.
